Codeforces itself

Why do Codeforces ranks have colours and names?

Your rating is a number. It goes up when you do better than people with a similar rating, and down when you do worse.

This is the idea of chess ratings (the Elo system): beating a strong player gives you more points.

The colour shows the level at a glance, everywhere your handle appears.

Master, International Master and Grandmaster are titles borrowed from chess.

What this means for you: the goal of this guide, 1600, is Expert: blue.

What do "rated", "Div." and "hack" mean?

  • Rated: the contest changes your rating. An unrated one does not.
  • Div. (division): a contest for one range of ratings, like a league in sports. Div. 4 is rated below 1400, Div. 3 below 1600, Div. 2 below 2100, Div. 1 from 1900.
  • Hack: you send a test that breaks someone else's passing code.

In Div. 1 and Div. 2 rounds, you lock your solution, then read code in your room: a good hack gives +100, a wrong one −50.

Div. 3, Div. 4 and Educational rounds have 12 hours of hacking after the contest, with no points.

A hacked solution fails, even if it passed during the contest.

What this means for you: passing during the contest is not the end. Test the edge cases yourself.

Why does "Time limit exceeded" not say how close you were?

The judge runs your program and stops it when the time limit ends.

It does not wait to see when your program would finish. You may have needed 1.1 seconds, or a whole year. The judge cannot tell.

"Time limit exceeded on test 7" means: test 7 did not finish in time. The tests after it are not run.

So do not try to speed up an \( n^2 \) idea a little when \( n = 2 \cdot 10^5 \). Count the steps first.

What this means for you: after a TLE, count the steps for the biggest test. If they are far above \( 10^8 \), change the idea, not the details.

Why does passing the samples prove so little?

The samples are 1 to 3 small tests. They show you the input format and one or two answers.

The real tests are many: the biggest \( n \), edge cases and random numbers. A problem often has dozens of tests.

The samples almost never test overflow, speed or \( n = 1 \).

What this means for you: before you submit, try your own tests: the smallest, the biggest, and all values equal.
