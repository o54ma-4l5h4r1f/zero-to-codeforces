The judge does about \( 10^8 \) simple steps in one second. Count the steps of your idea before you write code.

Before this lesson

  • You know the verdict "Time limit exceeded" → How Codeforces works

  • You can read a loop over an array → Your C++ template

Use it on every problem

  1. Find the biggest \( n \) in the constraints.
  2. Count the steps of your idea for that \( n \).
  3. About \( 10^8 \) or less: fast enough. \( 10^9 \) or more: too slow, so find a better idea.

A "simple step" is one small action: add two numbers, compare two numbers, read one array cell. The number \( 10^8 \) is a safe estimate, not an exact law.

Count with numbers first

Take an array of \( n = 1000 \) numbers.

  • One loop over the array does 1000 steps.
  • A loop inside a loop does \( 1000 \cdot 1000 = 10^6 \) steps.

For any \( n \) these are \( n \) steps and \( n \cdot n = n^2 \) steps. We write them as \( O(n) \) and \( O(n^2) \).

\( O(\dots) \) means "about this many steps". Only the rough size matters, not the exact count. So \( n^2 / 2 \) steps and \( 3 n^2 \) steps are both written \( O(n^2) \). This rough size is called the complexity.

Four more symbols appear in this lesson:

SymbolMeaningExample
\( \log n \)How many times you cut \( n \) in half to reach 1.\( \log 8 = 3 \), because 8 → 4 → 2 → 1
\( \sqrt{n} \)The number that gives \( n \) when multiplied by itself.\( \sqrt{100} = 10 \)
\( 2^n \)The number 2 multiplied \( n \) times.\( 2^{10} = 1024 \)
\( n! \)\( 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \dots \cdot n \)\( 4! = 24 \)

Three ways to understand it

1 · Story — walk or fly

You must travel 1000 km and you have one day. You can walk, drive or fly. You do not start walking and hope. You look at the distance first, then you choose.

The limits are the distance. The time limit is your one day. \( O(n^2) \) is walking. \( O(n \log n) \) is driving. \( O(\log n) \) is flying.

2 · Picture — how many steps

\( n \)\( \log n \)\( n \log n \)\( n^2 \)
10about 3about 33100
1000about 10about \( 10^4 \)\( 10^6 \)
\( 10^5 \)about 17about \( 1.7 \cdot 10^6 \)\( 10^{10} \): about 100 seconds

For small \( n \) every idea is fast. For \( n = 10^5 \) the \( n^2 \) idea gets "Time limit exceeded".

3 · Code — count the loops

\( O(n) \): one loop.


long long sum = 0;
for (int i = 0; i < n; i++) sum += a[i];          // n steps

\( O(n^2) \): a loop inside a loop. This is a nested loop.


int pairs = 0;
for (int i = 0; i < n; i++)
    for (int j = i + 1; j < n; j++)               // about n * n / 2 steps
        if (a[i] == a[j]) pairs++;

\( O(\log n) \): the number is cut in half each time.


int steps = 0;
while (n > 1) {                                   // 10^9 -> 1 in about 30 steps
    n /= 2;
    steps++;
}

The key table

Read the biggest \( n \) in the problem. The table tells you which speed you need.

Biggest \( n \)Speed that fitsSteps for that \( n \)How the code looks
\( n \le 10 \)\( O(n!) \)about \( 4 \cdot 10^6 \)Try every order.
\( n \le 20 \)\( O(2^n) \)about \( 10^6 \)Try every "take it or not".
\( n \le 500 \)\( O(n^3) \)about \( 10^8 \)Three loops, one inside the other.
\( n \le 5000 \)\( O(n^2) \)\( 2.5 \cdot 10^7 \)Two loops: all pairs.
\( n \le 2 \cdot 10^5 \) (or \( 10^6 \))\( O(n \log n) \) or \( O(n) \)about \( 4 \cdot 10^6 \)One loop. Or sort first, then one loop.
\( n \le 10^{12} \)\( O(\sqrt{n}) \)\( 10^6 \)A loop while \( i \cdot i \le n \).
\( n \le 10^{18} \)\( O(\log n) \) or \( O(1) \)about 60A formula, or cutting in half.

The row \( n \le 2 \cdot 10^5 \) is the most common one. It means: two nested loops over \( n \) are too slow.

\( O(1) \) means the number of steps does not grow with \( n \).

Solve one together

Problem. You get \( n \) numbers. Choose two of them and subtract one from the other. Print the biggest result you can get. Limits: \( 2 \le n \le 2 \cdot 10^5 \), and every number is from 1 to \( 10^9 \).

  1. What is asked? Among all pairs of numbers, the biggest value of (one number) minus (the other number).
  2. A small example by hand. The numbers are 3, 9, 4, 1. Some pairs: \( 9 - 3 = 6 \), \( 4 - 1 = 3 \), \( 9 - 1 = 8 \). The best is 8. It uses the biggest number and the smallest number.
  3. The idea. First idea: try every pair with two nested loops. Count the steps for the biggest \( n \): \[ (2 \cdot 10^5)^2 = 4 \cdot 10^{10} \text{ steps} \]

    That is about 400 seconds. Too slow. Better idea: the answer is always the biggest number minus the smallest number. One loop finds both.

  4. Why is it fast enough? One loop does \( 2 \cdot 10^5 \) steps. That is far below \( 10^8 \). The speed is \( O(n) \).

#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

int main() {
    int n;
    cin >> n;
    vector<int> a(n);
    for (int i = 0; i < n; i++) cin >> a[i];

    int biggest = a[0], smallest = a[0];
    for (int i = 1; i < n; i++) {              // one loop: n steps
        if (a[i] > biggest) biggest = a[i];
        if (a[i] < smallest) smallest = a[i];
    }
    cout << biggest - smallest << "\n";
}

Rule: put the biggest \( n \) into your \( O(\dots) \). Result \( \le 10^8 \) → OK for 1 second.

With t test cases

Many problems have \( t \) test cases in one input. Look for one line in the statement.

  • The statement says "the sum of \( n \) over all test cases is at most \( 2 \cdot 10^5 \)": use that sum as your \( n \). An \( O(n) \) solution for each test is fine.
  • The statement does not say it: multiply. Steps = \( t \times \) (steps for one test). Example: \( t = 10^4 \) tests with \( n = 2 \cdot 10^5 \) each give \( 2 \cdot 10^9 \) steps.

Wrong ideas people get

  • You may think: My code answers the samples at once, so it is fast enough.

    The truth: The samples are very small. The judge also tests the biggest \( n \). Count the steps for that \( n \).

  • You may think: An \( O(n^2) \) solution is always bad.

    The truth: For \( n \le 5000 \) it is fine. Small limits mean: the simple way is enough. Do not write a hard, fast solution when \( n \le 100 \).

  • You may think: \( O(n) \) means exactly \( n \) steps.

    The truth: It means "about \( n \)": maybe \( n / 2 \), maybe \( 5 n \). So you compare with \( 10^8 \) only roughly.

Common mistakes

  • You write two nested loops for \( n = 2 \cdot 10^5 \). That is \( 4 \cdot 10^{10} \) steps.
  • You call a function inside a loop. If the function has its own loop, you have a nested loop.
  • You clear an array of size \( 10^6 \) in every test case. With \( t = 10^4 \) that is \( 10^{10} \) steps.

Check yourself

  1. \( n \le 2 \cdot 10^5 \) and your idea has two nested loops over \( n \). Is it fast enough?
    Answer

    No. It does about \( 4 \cdot 10^{10} \) steps. You need \( O(n \log n) \) or \( O(n) \).

  2. \( n \le 1000 \) and your idea has three nested loops over \( n \). Is it fast enough?
    Answer

    No. \( 1000^3 = 10^9 \) steps. That is too slow. Two loops would be fine: \( 10^6 \) steps.

  3. There are \( t \le 10^4 \) tests. The sum of \( n \) over all tests is at most \( 2 \cdot 10^5 \). Your solution is \( O(n) \) for each test. About how many steps in total?
    Answer

    About \( 2 \cdot 10^5 \) steps. You use the sum as \( n \). You do not multiply by \( t \).
