Use it on every problem

  1. Find the biggest \( n \) in the constraints.
  2. Look it up in the key table below. It gives the \( O(\dots) \) that fits.
  3. Look for an idea with that speed or faster. Then cost your code before you submit.

You already know the rule: about \( 10^8 \) simple steps per second. This lesson turns it around: from the limits to the speed.

One new symbol

\( n! \) means \( 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \dots \cdot n \). Example: \( 4! = 24 \). It is the number of ways to put \( n \) things in a row. \( \log n \), \( \sqrt{n} \) and \( 2^n \) you know from the last lesson.

Two ways to see it

1 · Story — walk or fly

You must travel 1000 km and you have one day. You can walk, drive or fly. You do not start walking and hope. You look at the distance first, then you choose.

The limits are the distance. The time limit is your one day. \( O(n^2) \) is walking. \( O(n \log n) \) is driving. \( O(\log n) \) is flying.

\( 2^n \) leaves the chart before \( n = 50 \). That is why \( O(2^n) \) works only for \( n \le 20 \).

The key table

Read the biggest \( n \) in the problem. The table tells you which speed you need.

The row \( n \le 2 \cdot 10^5 \) is the most common one. It means: two nested loops over \( n \) are too slow.

\( O(1) \) means the number of steps does not grow with \( n \).

Solve one together

Problem. You get \( n \) numbers. Choose two of them and subtract one from the other. Print the biggest result you can get. Limits: \( 2 \le n \le 2 \cdot 10^5 \), and every number is from 1 to \( 10^9 \).

  1. What is asked? Among all pairs of numbers, the biggest value of (one number) minus (the other number).
  2. A small example by hand. The numbers are 3, 9, 4, 1. Some pairs: \( 9 - 3 = 6 \), \( 4 - 1 = 3 \), \( 9 - 1 = 8 \). The best is 8. It uses the biggest number and the smallest number.
  3. The idea. First idea: try every pair with two nested loops. Count the steps for the biggest \( n \): \[ (2 \cdot 10^5)^2 = 4 \cdot 10^{10} \text{ steps} \]

    That is about 400 seconds. Too slow. Better idea: the answer is always the biggest number minus the smallest number. One loop finds both.

  4. Why is it fast enough? One loop does \( 2 \cdot 10^5 \) steps. That is far below \( 10^8 \). The speed is \( O(n) \).

#include <bits/stdc++.h>
using namespace std;

int main() {
    int n;
    cin >> n;
    vector<int> a(n);
    for (int i = 0; i < n; i++) cin >> a[i];

    int biggest = a[0], smallest = a[0];
    for (int i = 1; i < n; i++) {              // one loop: n steps
        if (a[i] > biggest) biggest = a[i];
        if (a[i] < smallest) smallest = a[i];
    }
    cout << biggest - smallest << "\n";
}

Rule: put the biggest \( n \) into your \( O(\dots) \). Result \( \le 10^8 \) → OK for 1 second.

Many test cases? Use the sum of \( n \) from the statement as your \( n \). See How much does my code cost?

Wrong ideas people get

  • You may think: My code answers the samples at once, so it is fast enough.

    The truth: The samples are very small. The judge also tests the biggest \( n \). Count the steps for that \( n \).

  • You may think: An \( O(n^2) \) solution is always bad.

    The truth: For \( n \le 5000 \) it is fine. Small limits mean: the simple way is enough. Do not write a hard, fast solution when \( n \le 100 \).

  • You may think: The biggest number in the limits is my \( n \).

    The truth: \( n \) is how many items your loops go over, not how big the values are. Ten numbers up to \( 10^9 \) each are a tiny input.

Common mistakes

  • You write two nested loops for \( n = 2 \cdot 10^5 \). That is \( 4 \cdot 10^{10} \) steps.
  • You look only at \( n \) and forget the second size, like \( m \) or the number of questions \( q \).

Check yourself

  1. \( n \le 2 \cdot 10^5 \) and your idea has two nested loops over \( n \). Is it fast enough?
    Answer

    No. It does about \( 4 \cdot 10^{10} \) steps. You need \( O(n \log n) \) or \( O(n) \).

  2. \( n \le 1000 \) and your idea has three nested loops over \( n \). Is it fast enough?
    Answer

    No. \( 1000^3 = 10^9 \) steps. That is too slow. Two loops would be fine: \( 10^6 \) steps.

  3. \( n \le 10^{12} \). Which speed fits, and how does the code look?
    Answer

    \( O(\sqrt{n}) \) or faster: a loop while i * i <= n, about \( 10^6 \) rounds. A loop up to \( n \) does \( 10^{12} \) steps: too slow.
