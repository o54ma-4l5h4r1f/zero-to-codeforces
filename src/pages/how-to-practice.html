Solve a few problems a little above your level, every day. Think first, read the solution late, then write the code yourself.

The practice loop

  1. Pick a problem at your rating, or up to 200 above it. No rating yet? Start with 800.
  2. Think for 20–40 minutes. Use paper. Try small examples by hand.
  3. Stuck? Open the editorial. Read only until you get the idea. Then close it.
  4. Write the code yourself and get Accepted. Reading a solution is not solving.
  5. Write one line in your notebook: the trick you learned.

How to pick problems

Open the Problemset on Codeforces. Set the rating filter, for example 800 to 1000. Then sort by the number of people who solved each problem.

The problem feels…What it meansWhat to do

impossible, even with the editorialtoo far for nowgo down by 100–200

More solvers = a fairer problem for its rating. Start with the most-solved problems. Leave the strange ones for later.

The practice ladders in this guide are built the same way. They are sorted by the official rating, then by how many people solved each problem.

Three rules that matter most

1 · A little every day

3–5 problems a day is better than 30 problems on one day. Your brain learns while you sleep. Make practice a habit, like brushing your teeth.

2 · Upsolve after every contest

Upsolving means: after the contest, solve the next problem, the first one you could not solve. This one problem teaches you more than ten easy ones. It is exactly at the edge of your level.

3 · Never copy a solution

If you copy code, you get a green "Accepted" and learn nothing. Understand the idea, close the page, and type every line yourself. If you cannot, you did not understand it yet.

Contests

  • Join every Div. 4, Div. 3 and Educational round. They are made for learning. Join Div. 2 rounds too when you have time.
  • No contest at a good time for you? Use virtual participation: an old contest with a real timer. It does not change your rating.
  • In a contest, solve in order: A, then B, then C. Do not stay on one problem for the whole time.
  • Your rating goes up and down. This is normal. Ignore it for your first 20 contests. Count solved problems, not rating points.

Common mistakes

  • You solve only easy problems because green feels good. You stop growing.
  • You open the editorial after 5 minutes. The thinking time is the training.
  • You read the editorial and say "I understand", but you do not write the code.
  • You watch lessons for weeks and solve nothing. One lesson, then problems.
